Day by Day

What to See, Where to Sleep

Every day: key sites with entry prices, three accommodation tiers, and the nearest onsen. Tap a day to expand.

Kyushu — Days 1–4
Day 1 Cape Sata → Kagoshima 95 km · +350m ▼
See & Do
  • Cape Sata Lighthouse — southernmost tip of Kyushu. Sign the start log, take the photo.
  • Ibusuki Sand Bath (砂むし温泉) — get buried in naturally heated volcanic sand on the beach · ¥1,100 · 30 min stop
  • Sengan-en Garden — 350-year-old shogun villa with Sakurajima as the backdrop · UNESCO World Heritage · ¥1,500
  • Sakurajima — active volcano views across Kagoshima Bay all day long

Day 2 ★ Kagoshima → Kirishima 80 km · +750m ▼
See & Do
  • Kirishima Jingu Shrine — Japan's oldest and most sacred shrine complex. Where the gods descended to earth according to Japanese mythology. Almost zero foreign tourists. Free.
  • Onami-ike Crater Lake — volcanic caldera lake near the shrine, stunning blue-green
  • Kirishima Range — volcanic peaks visible all day; dramatic highland riding

Day 3 Kirishima → Hitoyoshi 95 km · +1,000m ▼
See & Do
  • Ebino Plateau — volcanic highland with crater lakes and alpine views mid-route
  • Aoi Aso Shrine — one of Kumamoto's most ancient shrines, free, tranquil forest setting
  • Hitoyoshi Castle Ruins — on a hill above the Kuma River · 700-year Sagara clan seat · ¥220
  • Kuma River — one of Japan's three great rapid rivers; views from the bridge on arrival

Day 4 ★ Hitoyoshi → Oka Castle (Taketa) 105 km · +1,200m ▼
See & Do
  • Oka Castle Ruins — "Japan's most beautiful ruins." 1185 AD. Massive stone walls on a mountain ridge with no roof and no tourists. Free entry.
  • Taketa town — small Edo-era castle town at the base; explore the old streets
  • Nagayu Onsen (10km east) — Japan's most famous CO2-carbonated spring. Bathing in it feels like sparkling water on your skin. ¥300.

Day 11 ★ Wakayama → Koyasan 65 km · +950m ▼
See & Do
  • Okunoin Cemetery — walk it at dusk. 2km through ancient cedar forest lined with 200,000+ tombstones and memorials, lanterns lit continuously for over 1,000 years · free
  • Kongobuji Temple — head temple of Shingon Buddhism, rock garden the size of a stadium · ¥1,000
  • Danjo Garan — the original temple complex founded by Kobo Daishi in 819 AD, vermillion pagoda · ¥500

Day 13 ★ Nara → Kyoto (via Horyuji + Uji) 80 km · +400m ▼
See & Do
  • Todaiji (morning) — arrive before crowds, gets busy by 9am · ¥600
  • Horyuji Temple (607 AD) — the oldest wooden building on earth. 1,400 years old and still standing. Ancient Buddhas that predate most of the world's cathedrals · ¥1,500
  • Byodoin Temple, Uji — the temple on the 10-yen coin. Phoenix Hall, 1052 AD, floating on its reflection · ¥600
  • Ujigami Shrine — oldest shrine building in Japan (late 900s AD) · UNESCO · free
  • Uji matcha tea — Japan's finest matcha comes from here. Stop at any tea house on the main street · ¥800 tea ceremony

Day 16 ★ Rest Day — Nagoya REST Toyota Museum ▼
See & Do
  • Toyota Commemorative Museum of Industry and Technology — give it the full day. Starts with the original 1890s textile looms (Toyota started as a loom company), through early automobiles, to F1 engines. Japan's industrial revolution in one building. ¥500. Incredible.
  • Nagoya Castle — if you didn't go last night · ¥500
  • Atsuta Shrine — one of Shinto's most important shrines, said to house one of the three Imperial Regalia (the sacred sword Kusanagi) · free, if time allows
♨ Nearby Onsen
Dormy Inn Premium Nagoya onsen on-site. Or explore a neighborhood sento (public bath) · ¥400 — a slice of real local life.
Day 17 ★ Train to Gifu · Gifu → Takayama 100 km riding · +1,100m ▼
See & Do
  • Train: Nagoya → Gifu Station — ~20 min · ¥740. Skip the boring flat highway. Ride only the scenic part.
  • Gifu Castle — mountaintop castle, cable car up · ¥200 · views over the city and Nagara River
  • Ukai Cormorant Fishing — 1,300-year tradition on the Nagara River. Fishermen use trained cormorants on leashes to catch ayu fish, watched from wooden boats by lantern light. May–October evenings · ¥3,500. Book ahead.
  • Takayama Sanmachi Suji — the most perfectly preserved Edo merchant district in Japan. Breweries, sake shops, lacquerware, all in original buildings · free to walk
  • Jinya-mae Morning Market — if arriving early enough, or prep for tomorrow morning

Good to Know

Key Logistics

✈️

Getting There

Fly SLC → Tokyo, arrive Apr 29. Rest a day, then Shinkansen to Kagoshima (~7 hrs) and local transfer to Cape Sata. After the finish, return via Sapporo to Tokyo for 4 days, then fly home Jun 12–13. Note: Apr 29–May 5 is Golden Week — book Shinkansen seats and Tokyo hotels early.

🚲

Bikes on Trains

Bikes in Japan ride in a "rinko bag" (輪行袋) — partially disassembled, bagged. Generally free on local trains. Practice packing yours before you go.

🛂

Entry Requirements

US citizens get 90-day visa-free entry to Japan. No visa required. Just a valid passport and proof of onward travel.

🗺️

Navigation

Download offline maps: Komoot or Ride with GPS for route tracking. Google Maps for local navigation. Most major cycling routes have bilingual signs.

🌡️

May Weather

Start warm in Kyushu (18–27°C). By the time you reach Hokkaido in June it'll be 10–20°C. Layer up for morning starts — afternoons warm fast.

🏠

Rider Houses

Cheap cyclist-specific lodges common in Hokkaido. ¥1,500–3,000/night. Often include a kitchen and lounge. Search "ライダーハウス" + town name.

🏪

Convenience Stores

7-Eleven, FamilyMart, and Lawson are a cyclist's best friend. Hot food, drinks, ATMs, phone charging, and restrooms — usually within 10 km of anywhere.

♨️

Onsen

Budget ¥400–1,000 per soak. Aim for 10–15 throughout the trip. Essential recovery. Most are separated by gender — no swimwear, just you and the hot water.

🚨

Emergency

Japan emergency number: 119 (ambulance/fire) or 110 (police). English is spoken at major hospitals. Your travel insurance's emergency line is your first call.
